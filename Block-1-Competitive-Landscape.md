# Block 1 — Competitive Landscape Analysis

**Date:** April 29, 2026
**Purpose:** Map the competitive field FluentPath is entering. Identify gaps, threats, positioning options.
**Reading time:** ~15 min.

---

## Two markets, not one

The first thing to establish: you've been talking about competitors that live in **two different categories**.

**Category A — Exam prep platforms (TCF/TEF/DELF specifically).** Direct competitors to FluentPath's stated pitch. Buyers come with a known goal (pass exam X for outcome Y). Conversion is high because pain is concrete.

**Category B — General language learning apps.** Adjacent, not direct. They don't focus on exam prep but compete for the same time and money. Buyers come with a vaguer goal (learn French). Conversion is lower per visitor but volume is massive.

A third category matters more than you flagged:

**Category C — Test-prep platforms in OTHER subjects (Magoosh, Kaplan, TestPrep-Online, Union Test Prep).** Not French at all. But their product structure (video lessons + practice tests + dashboards + score guarantees) is the operational model worth studying. They're not competitors but they're the **product blueprint** for what TCF prep should look like in 2026.

## Direct competitor scan (Category A — exam-prep specific)

### PrepMyFuture — the incumbent

**What it is.** A Paris-based French e-learning platform. The dominant TCF prep service. Distributed widely through partnerships with Alliances Françaises across the world.

**Pricing.** $39 to $99 depending on distribution channel. Alliance Française NY sells it at $39. Alliance Calgary at $60. Alliance Edmonton at $65. Direct purchase on prepmyfuture.com lists at $99/€89. **One-time payment, 12-month access.**

**What's included** (TCF Canada package):
- 40 study sheets
- 3 full practice tests
- 1 mini-test (diagnostic)
- 1,000+ exercises
- Detailed answer explanations
- Bilingual (French/English)
- Personalized dashboard, progress tracking
- Refund window: 7 days, fewer than 100 questions completed

**What's NOT included** (this is the gap):
- No speaking practice / oral expression practice (the section the official TCF actually has — `expression orale`)
- No AI feedback on student-produced output
- No tutor integration
- No video lessons. It's text-based study sheets and MCQ
- No adaptive learning path
- No mobile app (web only, last I can verify)

**Why this matters for you:**
PrepMyFuture is the price floor. $39-$99 ONE-TIME is what TCF candidates expect to pay for "everything they need to prepare." You can't sell a $30/month subscription against a $39 one-time competitor unless your product is meaningfully different.

But also — they are doing four mandatory tests (listening, reading, written, oral) and **NOT actually preparing the oral section properly**. That's the seam.

### CrackTheTCFCanada

Smaller. Live tutor-based courses. $50/month referral discount. Targets the same Canadian PR market. Premium positioning ($$ unclear from their site but appears $200-400+ per course based on industry benchmarks). Risk-averse choice — these are tutoring services with a website, not products.

### TCFTEFprep.com — the pure-product competitor

A 2026-built, more recent platform. **Pay once, practice as long as you need.** Free tier exists (3 listening + 3 reading exercises + CLB orientation). Paid tier unlocks 20,000+ practice questions, mock exams, AI writing feedback. Mobile responsive.

**This is the most direct threat to FluentPath as a product.** They built an exam-focused, AI-assisted platform aimed at the same TCF Canada market in the same year. Worth studying their pricing page before you decide your own.

### Fluent Fast Academy (India)

Mentioned because they're aggressively targeting the Indian TCF/TEF market with live Zoom courses. 8-month or 10-month programs. Pricing not transparent on the site, but anecdotally $200-500 USD for a full course based on comparable academies. **High-touch, low-tech, instructor-led.** Different category — they sell hours, not software.

### Kwiziq French (kwiziq.com)

Adaptive grammar-focused platform. Free tier. Premium subscription. **Not TCF-focused** but used by TCF candidates to brush up. Their adaptive engine ("KwizBot") for diagnosing weak points is worth studying — the kind of feature a real prep platform should have.

### French-exam.com / TCF Exam Hub

Content marketing site. Free TCF prep blog. Sells study guides. Aggregates student questions and recreates them as content. Lower-tier competitor but does well in SEO. They already rank for many of the searches your potential users will run.

## Adjacent threats (Category B — general language apps)

### Promova (the one you study most)

**Why it matters.** You've explicitly used Promova as your visual benchmark. They have a polished, beginner-friendly app. 12 languages including French. **They don't do TCF prep**, but their UI sets the design bar.

**Pricing reality:**
- Web 6-month plan: ~$49.99 (sometimes promo'd lower)
- Annual plan: ~$83.99
- Lifetime: ~$79.99 (during promotions)
- Monthly: ~$19.99
- Group classes: $95.99/month for 16 lessons (~$6/lesson)
- Trustpilot has real complaints about subscription/billing dark patterns

**Threat level to you: low directly, high aspirationally.**
They will probably never enter TCF prep — too narrow for their general-purpose strategy. But they're who your users compare you to when evaluating "is this app well-designed and worth my money."

**Note for benchmarking:** their AI tutor + role-play scenario engine (cafe ordering, hotel check-in, embassy visa interviews) is their differentiator. They've nailed the conversational practice loop FluentPath wants.

### Natulang

Speech-centric language app. iOS + Android only. 13 languages. **No TCF prep.** Pricing: ~$100 lifetime (per user reviews on Apple Store). Standard subscription model with monthly/annual.

**Threat level: low.** Different audience entirely. They serve "I want to speak Spanish casually" not "I need 7/10 on TCF expression orale." Mentioned because you listed them, but they're not a real competitor.

### Babbel, Duolingo, Drops, Pimsleur, FluentU

The general-purpose category. Babbel ~$13/month, Duolingo free with $6.99/month Super tier, Drops free with limited free version, Pimsleur ~$19.95/month, FluentU ~$30/month.

**Threat level: very low directly.** None of them prep for TCF/TEF/DELF exams. But they're who an undecided buyer compares against — "should I pay $30/month for FluentPath, or just use Duolingo?"

This means **your sales narrative needs to explicitly contrast against Duolingo**. You're not in their game, but you have to say so loud enough that the buyer doesn't make an apples-to-oranges price comparison.

### Preply itself (your own marketplace)

The category-killer that you operate within. Preply tutors charge $15-50/hr, with serious tutors averaging $25-35/hr. **Their entire model is selling human time, scaled by liquidity rather than product.** Some tutors specialize in TCF/TEF/DELF.

**Threat level: high in a specific way.** Preply IS your alternative offering — a TCF candidate can either buy your product OR buy 5-10 hours with a Preply tutor for similar money. Your differentiator can't be "I'm cheaper than tutoring." It has to be "I unlock something tutoring can't (24/7 unlimited practice, instant feedback, structured curriculum)."

## Operational blueprints to learn from (Category C — non-French test prep)

### Magoosh (the gold standard of self-paced test prep)

**Why study them.** They built the best-in-class self-paced test prep platform model. GRE, GMAT, SAT, ACT, MCAT, TOEFL, IELTS, LSAT. 1.5M+ users since 2008.

**Pricing.** GRE: $99/month or $129 for 6 months. TOEFL: ~$129 for 6 months. SAT/ACT: $129 self-study or $399 with live classes. **MUCH higher than PrepMyFuture for comparable test prep.**

**What makes them work:**
- 8-minute video lessons (not text — VIDEO)
- Score increase guarantee (+5 GRE, +10 MCAT, or money back)
- 7-day free trial, no credit card required
- 250+ expert-led video clips
- Adaptive practice questions
- Mobile app (offline mode for subway studying)
- AI tutor for asking questions
- Score predictor based on practice performance
- 4.8/5 average rating

**Lessons for FluentPath:**
- Video lessons command 4-5x higher prices than text platforms
- Score guarantees are powerful conversion tools
- Free trial lowers friction massively
- Mobile-first matters — 73% of Magoosh users study on the go

### Kaplan (the high-touch, high-cost model)

**Why study them.** They're the legacy player. Their products cost $449-$2,000+. They sell live classes, tutoring, and self-paced.

**What makes them work for the wealthy buyer:**
- Live classroom experience
- Personal tutor matching
- 90+ test prep products
- Brand trust ("Kaplan" carries weight in admissions)

**Lessons for FluentPath:**
- The premium tier at 5-10x the price exists. Some buyers prefer it. Hybrid product can serve both.

### Union Test Prep (the freemium-with-classroom model)

**Why study them.** Free + Premium model. ~$30-50/year for premium. **Has a tutor classroom feature** that lets tutors invite students, track progress, manage groups.

**Why this matters specifically for you:**
This is the **ONLY model on the list that explicitly supports tutor-led classrooms**. If your "human + AI completeness" thesis is real, this is the closest operational pattern that exists. You could:
- Sell direct to TCF candidates (B2C)
- AND sell a "tutor classroom" tier where French tutors invite their own students into FluentPath as a study tool
- Tutors get insights, students get structured practice
- Network effect: more tutors → more students → more data → better product

**This is the most strategically interesting finding in the entire competitive scan.**

## Pattern recognition — what the field actually looks like

After scanning everyone, three honest observations:

### 1. The TCF prep market is small, fragmented, and bad

PrepMyFuture is the only real platform with scale. Everything else is either tutoring marketplaces (Preply) or content-marketing sites (french-exam.com, TCF Exam Hub). **None of them are using AI for speaking practice with quality feedback.** None use video instruction. None do adaptive learning. PrepMyFuture's product is from 2018 in feel — it's a quiz database with study sheets.

This is good news for you. The gap is real.

### 2. The general language app market is saturated and aesthetic

Promova, Babbel, Duolingo, Pimsleur, Drops — all of them have polished UI, mobile apps, gamification, AI tutors. **The aesthetic bar is set high but not insurmountable.** You can match it with a focused design pass.

The competition you're seeing as "very aesthetic and clean" is largely Category B, not Category A. PrepMyFuture (your real competitor) is genuinely ugly. **You don't need to beat Promova to dominate TCF prep. You need to beat PrepMyFuture, and clear the visual quality bar Promova set.**

### 3. The high-priced model exists alongside the low-priced model

Magoosh sells test prep for $99-$399. PrepMyFuture sells TCF prep for $39. Kaplan sells for $700-$2,000. **The market doesn't have a single price point.** The price you can charge depends on the value framing, not the category default.

## What this means for FluentPath positioning

Five viable positions you could take:

### Position A — "Better PrepMyFuture" (price-similar, feature-superior)
Sell a one-time package at $49-89 that beats PrepMyFuture on:
- Speaking practice with AI feedback (the section they ignore)
- Modern UI
- Adaptive learning path
- Mobile-first
**Risk:** Race to the bottom on price. Low LTV.

### Position B — "Magoosh of TCF" (premium subscription, score guarantee)
Sell a $19-29/month subscription with:
- Video lessons (you record them — Rec 3)
- Score guarantee
- Mobile + web
- AI tutor + speaking lab
- Weekly office hours with you (Chadi as the human)
**Risk:** Higher production cost, requires you to record courses, slower to ship.

### Position C — "Tutor-in-a-box" (B2B + B2C hybrid)
Sell to French tutors as a teaching tool. Tutors invite students. Tutors get dashboard + insights. Students get structured practice.
- Tutor tier: $30/month per tutor, includes 10 student seats
- Direct B2C tier: $19/month
**Risk:** Two-sided market, harder to grow. But the most strategic — you become Preply's complement, not competitor.

### Position D — "Coursera for French exams" (full platform)
The vision you described. Video courses, downloadable documents, tree-like adaptive paths, exam simulators. Premium pricing ($30-50/month).
**Risk:** 6-12 months to build. Burns runway. But the most defensible long-term.

### Position E — "Speaking lab + add-ons" (start narrow, expand)
What you have right now is essentially Position E. Speaking practice with intelligent feedback. Sell at $14.99/month. Add Writing, Progress, Exam Sim, Video courses one at a time as features.
**Risk:** Looks thin at launch. May lose to PrepMyFuture on perceived completeness.

## My honest read

You have **three real strategic edges** competitors don't:

1. **TCF/TEF/DELF expertise from 7,000 tutoring hours.** This is actually rare. Most platforms are built by generic edtech founders, not domain experts. Your pedagogy IS the moat — Les Moules, L1 interference framework, the rubric you've built.

2. **You're a real human who can be the face.** Promova/Magoosh/PrepMyFuture have no founder presence. You can do TikTok/YouTube/Instagram in French and English and Arabic, attracting Moroccan + Algerian + Tunisian + Indian + Vietnamese candidates directly. Your origin story matters.

3. **You can do a hybrid AI + human product that pure-play AI competitors can't.** This is what you said yesterday. Promova is AI-only. Preply is human-only. FluentPath could be both.

You have **two real strategic disadvantages**:

1. **Solo founder, limited time.** Every hour spent building means an hour not tutoring, not marketing, not recording courses.

2. **No marketing budget yet.** You can't outspend Promova on ads. You need organic growth (content, referrals, Preply community) which is slower.

## Naming/branding — direct answer

**Confidence: high.** You should NOT keep "FluentPath."

Three reasons:
1. There's an existing organization using the name (per your earlier note)
2. ".io" is fine but ".com" at $4,400 is a weak signal investment for a pre-revenue product
3. The name is generic — could be ANY language platform. Doesn't telegraph French exam specialty.

**Real options worth exploring:**
- A French word that signals exam confidence (e.g., "Maîtrise," "Réussir," "Le Cap")
- A reference to TCF/TEF/DELF directly (e.g., "TCFCoach," "DelfMaster")
- A founder-led brand using your name (e.g., "Chadi.fr" — risky if you sell, attractive while solo)
- A pun on the test name (e.g., "TCF Lab," "PassTheTCF")

This deserves its own decision-making block. I'd address it after Block 4.
