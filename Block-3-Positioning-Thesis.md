## The positioning sentence

I'm going to give you three candidates, then a recommendation. You react.

> **"[BRAND] is the TCF speaking coach that gets you to NCLC 7."**

**Pros:** Specific outcome. Specific exam. Specific score. Names the role (coach, not app).

### Candidate 2 — Method-anchored

> **"[BRAND] turns 7,000 hours of French exam pedagogy into 24/7 personal training for your speaking section."**

> **"[BRAND] is what PrepMyFuture left out — the speaking section, finally trained right."**

**Pros:** Names the competitor explicitly. Makes the gap the story. "Finally" carries emotional weight (frustration with existing options). Buyer instantly understands what's different.
**Cons:** Picks a fight with PrepMyFuture (which I think is correct now that we know clients pick one). Won't read well to buyers who've never heard of PrepMyFuture.

### My recommendation

**Use Candidate 3 as the headline on the landing page.** Use Candidate 1 as the social media one-liner / SEO meta description. Use Candidate 2 as the "About" section copy.

Three positions, three contexts, one underlying thesis. Confidence: medium-high.

## The brand promise

**The brand promise is what [BRAND] commits to deliver.** It's narrower than the positioning sentence but more concrete.

Three options, in order of boldness:

### Promise A — Confidence promise (safe)

> "We promise you'll feel ready for the TCF speaking section by exam day."

**Pros:** Defensible. Can't be litigated. Easy to deliver.
**Cons:** Soft. "Feel ready" is vague. Doesn't differentiate.

### Promise B — Skill promise (medium)

> "We promise you'll know exactly what to say in all 3 TCF speaking tasks — and how to say it like a B2-level French speaker."

**Pros:** Specific (3 tasks, B2 level). Directly addresses what TCF candidates fear (blanking out, sounding wrong). Marketable in concrete terms.
**Cons:** Doesn't promise score. Buyers might still wonder "but will I pass?"

### Promise C — Score promise (bold)

> "We promise you'll improve at least 1 NCLC level on your TCF Canada speaking score, or your money back."

**Pros:** Specific outcome. Magoosh-style score guarantee. Instant trust signal.
**Cons:** Requires data calibration (Phase 2-3). Can't make this promise on launch.

### My recommendation — phased

**Phase 1 launch (now):** Promise B. Concrete, deliverable, marketable. Doesn't require infrastructure.

**Phase 2-3 (after data):** Promise C. Becomes the conversion weapon once you have evidence.

**You react.** Confidence: high on phasing logic. Medium-high on Promise B copy specifically.

## Where [BRAND] sits on the human-AI spectrum

This is the crucial positioning move. Get this wrong and the product looks like every other AI app.

### The spectrum

```
PURE AI                                                      PURE HUMAN
←——————————————————————————————————————————————————————→
FluentLab    Promova    Duolingo    [BRAND]    Magoosh    Preply    Tutor
(no human)  (AI tutor)  (no human)  (HYBRID)   (videos+   (humans   (human
                                                AI hints)  on demand) only)
```

### Where [BRAND] fits — concretely

**[BRAND] is not on the AI side. It's not on the human side. It's deliberately in the middle.**

The product is structured as:
- **AI as the practice partner** — 24/7 availability, infinite patience, instant feedback
- **Chadi as the curriculum architect** — the path is designed by a human, not generated by AI
- **Chadi as the content creator** — videos, written explanations, frameworks come from a real teacher
- **Chadi as the failsafe** — VIP tier offers real Zoom sessions when AI hits its limit
- **AI as the scale mechanism** — what tutors charge $25/hr to do, AI does for $19.99/month

### The positioning shorthand

**Not "AI tutor." Not "AI app." [BRAND] is a "coach with an AI assistant."**

This single word change — "coach" not "tutor" — does five things:
1. Coaches charge premium prices ($50-200/hr); tutors don't
2. Coaches deliver outcomes; tutors deliver lessons
3. Coaches use systems; tutors improvise
4. Coaches are accountable; tutors are scheduled
5. Coaches are trusted; tutors are interchangeable

**Buyers don't pay $19.99/month for "AI."** They pay for **Chadi's coaching, scaled by AI to be affordable.**

## La Méthode — your weapon

You have three named frameworks. They're moats. They're also marketing weapons.

- **Les Moules** (Sentence Molds) — for sentence architecture
- **Les Moules des Idées** (Idea Molds) — for B2+ rhetoric and argument architecture
- **La Méthode en Couches** (4-Layer Method) — for evaluating speaking across Le Fond / Les Moules des Idées / Les Moules / Les Réflexes Anglais
- **La Méthode Triple Action** — your proprietary teaching system

**Most AI competitors don't have ANY named methodology.** They have generic AI feedback and gamification. You have a named, structured, defensible pedagogy.

**Use the names everywhere:**

- Diagnostic page → "Voici votre score selon La Méthode en Couches"
- Module recommendations → "Cette leçon cible Les Moules niveau intermédiaire"
- Marketing → "La Méthode Chadi Bakhay, basée sur 7 000 heures d'enseignement"

**Naming the method makes it ownable.** Generic AI is commodity. Your method is brand.

This is one of the lightest-effort, highest-impact moves you can make. **Confidence: high.**

## What [BRAND] is NOT

Equally important. Clarity on rejection sharpens everything.

[BRAND] is NOT:

- **Not a French learning app.** Promova/Duolingo do that. We do exam prep.
- **Not a generic AI tutor.** FluentLab does that for IELTS. We're pedagogy with AI delivery.
- **Not a tutoring marketplace.** Preply does that. We're the structured coaching that complements (or partially replaces) tutors.
- **Not a content library.** PrepMyFuture is that. We're a coaching program with mock practice and AI feedback.
- **Not for casual learners.** We're for committed exam-takers with a date and a target score.
- **Not free forever.** Trial is free. Real value is paid.

**Saying what [BRAND] is NOT eliminates buyer confusion and prevents wrong-fit users from churning.**
