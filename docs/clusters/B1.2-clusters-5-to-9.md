# B1.2 — Phase Document: The Pronoun System

**Path:** B1 → B2 (Visa-Urgent / TCF Canada)
**Phase:** B1.2 — Pronoun Automation
**Clusters in this document:** Cluster 5 (COD), Cluster 6 (COI), Cluster 7 (Y and EN), Cluster 8 (Double pronouns), Cluster 9 (Pronoms toniques)
**Format:** In-app markdown lesson + structured exercises + Tâche-shaped practice prompt + detection rubric
**Author:** Chadi Bakhay (drafted by Co-PM Claude)
**Status:** Draft v0.1
**Prerequisites:** Phase B1.1 complete (Clusters 1-4) at status Absorbed or Partial

---

## Why this phase is the second-hardest leap toward B2

Cluster 1 told you the past system is the spine of B1→B2 narration. The pronoun system is the spine of B1→B2 **fluency**. The two systems are the only ones where a B1 student's surface grammar can look fine while production reveals deep gaps.

Specifically: **anglophones avoid French pronouns.** They produce technically grammatical but wooden sentences:

> ❌ *Je donne le livre à mon frère.*
> ✅ *Je le lui donne.* (or, in flowing speech, *Je le lui donne, à mon frère.*)

The first version is correct. It's also a B1 ceiling marker. A B2 speaker doesn't repeat full noun phrases — they pronominalize. A student who never says `le lui`, `m'en`, `vous y`, `t'y` is a student who hasn't yet acquired conversational French.

This phase is structured to break the avoidance pattern.

## Why all five clusters live in one document

The pronoun system is a single interlocking grammar. You can't teach COD without COI without inviting confusion. You can't teach double pronouns without y/en. You can't teach toniques without showing where the unstressed forms fail.

If you treat these as five separate clusters in five separate weeks, the student learns each one in isolation and never integrates. By document-level treatment, the student sees the system as a system. They learn cluster 5, then cluster 6 immediately confronts them with cluster 5's structures, and so on.

The order is sequenced by dependency:

1. **Cluster 5 — COD** (the most concrete: replaces direct objects, the noun-pronoun mapping is most intuitive)
2. **Cluster 6 — COI** (builds on COD: same idea, different grammatical role)
3. **Cluster 7 — Y and EN** (the two unique-to-French pronouns, acquired only after COD/COI are stable)
4. **Cluster 8 — Double pronouns** (combines all of the above; impossible to teach earlier)
5. **Cluster 9 — Toniques** (a different category; corrective layer that fixes errors students make in 5-8)

# CLUSTER 5 — COD: Direct Object Pronouns

```
Cluster ID:           B1.2-C5
Grammar topic:        Pronoms COD (le, la, les, l') — placement, agreement, with negation, with infinitives, with PC
Vocabulary theme:     Les relations / Les rapports humains
Tâche application:    Tâche 1 (description of someone) + Tâche 2 (responding about people)
Format:               In-app markdown
Estimated time:       60-75 minutes
Difficulty:           Foundational — the gateway to the entire pronoun system
```

## Why most students fail this in production

Anglophones know what COD pronouns are. They can identify them on a worksheet. They can fill in blanks. The failure happens at production time:

- The student needs 0.3 seconds to construct *je le vois* in conversation
- 0.3 seconds is too long under speaking pressure
- They default to *je vois Pierre* (using the noun)
- After enough repetitions of this default, the pronoun never automates

**The fix isn't more rules. It's more reps under pressure.** This cluster's exercises emphasize speaking-pace drilling, not reflective fill-in-the-blank.

## The L1 trap: English keeps the noun, French replaces it

In English, repeating a noun is normal:
> *I saw Pierre yesterday. I saw Pierre at the café.*

This is grammatically fine in English. It's marked but acceptable.

In French, repeating a noun is **socially marked as foreign or pedantic**:
> ❌ *J'ai vu Pierre hier. J'ai vu Pierre au café.*
> ✅ *J'ai vu Pierre hier. Je l'ai vu au café.*

A French speaker hearing the first version would not say "ungrammatical" — they'd say "you sound like a foreigner." The anglophone B1 ceiling is exactly this: grammatical but un-pronominalized.

## The mental model: COD = the noun that follows the verb directly

A direct object is a noun that follows the verb without a preposition:

| Sentence | Verb | Direct object | COD pronoun |
|---|---|---|---|
| Je vois **Pierre**. | vois | Pierre | le → *Je le vois.* |
| Tu manges **la pomme**. | manges | la pomme | la → *Tu la manges.* |
| Nous achetons **les billets**. | achetons | les billets | les → *Nous les achetons.* |
| Il aime **cette idée**. | aime | cette idée | la → *Il l'aime.* (élision before vowel) |

The forms: **le** (masculine sing.), **la** (feminine sing.), **les** (plural), **l'** (before vowel or silent h).

## Placement rules (the part that needs automation)

### Rule 1: Before the conjugated verb in simple tenses

> *Je le vois.* / *Tu la manges.* / *Nous les achetons.*

In English, the object pronoun comes after the verb (*I see him*). In French, before. Anglophones systematically misplace early.

### Rule 2: Before the auxiliary in compound tenses

> *Je l'ai vu.* / *Tu l'as mangée.* (note agreement) / *Nous les avons achetés.*

The pronoun goes before *avoir/être*, not before the past participle.

### Rule 3: Before the infinitive when the verb is followed by an infinitive

> *Je vais le voir.* / *Tu peux la manger.* / *Nous voulons les acheter.*

The pronoun attaches to the verb whose object it is. In *je vais voir Pierre*, "Pierre" is the object of *voir*, not of *aller*. So *je vais le voir* — pronoun before *voir*.

This is a B2-revealing detail. A B1 student often says *Je le vais voir* (incorrect) by mechanically placing the pronoun before the conjugated verb.

### Rule 4: Negation wraps around the verb-pronoun pair

> *Je ne le vois pas.* / *Tu ne l'as pas mangée.* / *Nous n'allons pas les acheter.*

Pronoun stays attached to its verb. Negation goes outside.

### Rule 5: Past participle agreement (the trap)

When COD precedes the past participle (which happens with ALL pronominalized direct objects in PC), the past participle agrees with it.

> *J'ai vu **les filles**.* (no agreement — COD comes after)
> *Je **les** ai **vues**.* (agreement: -es because *les* refers to feminine plural)

This is the most common B2-revealing mistake. The student says *Je les ai vu* (no agreement) — fluency-acceptable but exposes the gap.

**Calibration note:** Speed is the variable here. Detection rubric should track time-to-pronominalize in real recordings.

1. *Marie ?* Oui, je _______ (l'/la/l') ai _______ (vu/vue/vues) hier.

### Exercise 5.4 — Translate from English (force the L1 confrontation, 10 min)

2. He didn't recognize me. (*pre-exposure to first/second person COD: me/te*)
3. We're going to invite them next weekend.

6. I'm going to read this book tonight. (*place pronoun before correct verb*)

## Practice Prompt (Tâche 2 — Dialogue interactif, 5 min recording)

Tâche 2 is structurally a role-play where the candidate asks questions and responds to an examiner's prompts. This is exactly where COD avoidance shows up most aggressively.

> **« Vous parlez avec un nouveau collègue qui veut connaître votre quartier. Posez-lui cinq questions sur ses préférences (restaurants, lieux, activités), et répondez aux questions qu'il vous pose. Pendant l'échange, utilisez des pronoms autant que possible — ne répétez jamais le même nom deux fois de suite. »**
>
> Vous avez 1 minute pour préparer mentalement, puis 5 minutes d'échange avec l'examinateur.

This prompt is engineered to force pronominalization through a "no-repeat" instruction. The student who avoids pronouns will get caught immediately by the constraint.

## Detection Rubric (engine-facing)

### Quantitative signals

| Signal | Computation | Threshold |
|---|---|---|
| COD usage rate | `count(COD_pronouns) / count(potential_COD_contexts)` in 5-min recording | Healthy: >0.50. Below 0.30 = "COD avoidance" |
| Noun repetition rate | Same noun appearing 3+ times within 5 sentences when pronominalization was possible | Any occurrence = "pronoun avoidance flag" |
| Placement errors | COD placed AFTER conjugated verb (anglophone error: *Je vois le*) | Per-occurrence flag |
| Infinitive placement errors | COD placed before conjugated verb instead of before infinitive (*Je le vais voir*) | Per-occurrence flag |
| PC agreement errors | COD precedes PC but past participle doesn't agree | Per-occurrence flag (lower severity) |

### Qualitative markers (B1 ceiling)

- **Marker B1.2.C5.a — "COD avoidance":** student repeats nouns instead of pronominalizing in 50%+ of contexts
- **Marker B1.2.C5.b — "Placement transfer":** anglophone post-verb placement (*Je vois le*) firing in any utterance
- **Marker B1.2.C5.c — "Infinitive misplacement":** *Je le vais voir* pattern firing
- **Marker B1.2.C5.d — "PC agreement absence":** all pronominalized PC verbs missing agreement (lower severity, often last to fix)
- **Marker B1.2.C5.e — "First/second person avoidance":** student uses 3rd person COD (le/la/les) but avoids me/te in production

### Cluster status assignment

| Status | Conditions |
|---|---|
| **Absorbed** | COD usage rate >0.50. Markers C5.a–c silent. Marker C5.d firing in <30% of PC pronominalizations is acceptable (most B2 speakers still slip here). |
| **Partial** | COD usage rate 0.30–0.50. Marker C5.b or C5.c firing occasionally. |
| **Needs revisit** | COD usage rate <0.30 OR C5.a alone OR C5.b/C5.c firing in >50% of contexts. |

# CLUSTER 6 — COI: Indirect Object Pronouns

```
Cluster ID:           B1.2-C6
Grammar topic:        Pronoms COI (lui, leur) — placement and verb-COI matching
Vocabulary theme:     La communication / Les échanges sociaux
Tâche application:    Tâche 2 (dialogue), Tâche 3 (narrating interactions)
Format:               In-app markdown
Estimated time:       45-60 minutes
Difficulty:           Intermediate — placement is identical to COD, but verb-COI matching is its own learning curve
Prerequisites:        Cluster B1.2-C5 (COD)
```

## Why this cluster is shorter

Once COD is automated, COI is a reuse: same placement rules, same agreement-with-PC issues (well, almost — PC doesn't agree with COI), only the form changes. The real learning is **which verbs take COI** instead of COD.

## The L1 trap: English doesn't distinguish COD from COI cleanly

English uses preposition "to" or none at all:
- *I gave it to him.* (with preposition)
- *I gave him it.* (without preposition — same meaning)
- *I told him.* (no preposition, but it's still indirect)
- *I called him.* (no preposition, this is direct in English but in French is INDIRECT: *Je lui ai téléphoné*)

The trap: English speakers map "I told him" → *Je lui ai dit* (correct, because *dire* takes COI in French) but also map "I called him" → ❌ *Je l'ai appelé* (using COD) when French uses COI for *téléphoner*.

The verbs to learn:

**Verbs that take COI in French (COD in English or ambiguous):**
- téléphoner à — *Je lui téléphone* (NOT *Je l'appelle* if the meaning is "call on phone")
- répondre à — *Il lui répond*
- parler à — *Elle lui parle*
- écrire à — *Nous lui écrivons*
- demander à — *Tu lui demandes*
- ressembler à — *Elle lui ressemble*
- plaire à — *Le film lui plaît*
- manquer à — *Tu lui manques*
- obéir à — *L'enfant lui obéit*

**Verbs that take COD in French (where English speakers might say "to"):**
- regarder — *Je le regarde* (NOT *Je le regarde à lui*)
- écouter — *Je l'écoute* (NOT *Je l'écoute à lui*)
- chercher — *Je le cherche*
- attendre — *Je l'attends*

These two lists are the foundation. A B2 speaker has them automated. A B1 student guesses.

## Placement: same as COD

> *Je lui parle.* / *Je lui ai parlé.* / *Je vais lui parler.* / *Je ne lui parle pas.*

One important difference: **COI does NOT trigger past participle agreement** in PC.

> *Je lui ai parlé.* (no agreement — COI never agrees)
> *Je l'ai vu(e).* (COD: agreement with the noun pronominalized)

This actually simplifies things. Anglophones who fight COD agreement breathe easy here.

# CLUSTER 7 — Y and EN: The Two Pronouns Anglophones Avoid

```
Cluster ID:           B1.2-C7
Grammar topic:        Pronouns Y (replaces "à + thing/place") and EN (replaces "de + thing", quantities, partitives)
Vocabulary theme:     Les habitudes / La géographie quotidienne
Tâche application:    Tâche 1 + Tâche 2 (any context with quantities, places, opinions)
Format:               In-app markdown
Estimated time:       60-75 minutes
Difficulty:           Intermediate — these pronouns have no English equivalent, hence systematic avoidance
Prerequisites:        Clusters 5 + 6 (COD/COI placement automated)
```

## Why this cluster is the third pillar

Y and EN are the two pronouns anglophones avoid most aggressively, because **they have no English equivalent**. The student's brain finds nothing to map them to, so the brain doesn't reach for them.

The result: a student who says
- ❌ *Je vais à Paris.* / *Je vais à Paris souvent.* / *Quand est-ce que tu vas à Paris ?*

instead of
- ✅ *Je vais à Paris.* / *J'y vais souvent.* / *Quand est-ce que tu y vas ?*

Six words become two. Repetition disappears. The sentence breathes. **This is the difference between B1 production and B2 production in many real recordings.**

## What Y replaces

### Y replaces "à + thing or place"

> *Je vais à Paris.* → *J'y vais.*
> *Tu penses à ton avenir ?* → *Tu y penses ?*
> *Elle s'intéresse à la politique.* → *Elle s'y intéresse.*
> *Nous habitons à Casablanca.* → *Nous y habitons.*

Note: Y does NOT replace "à + person." For people, you use COI (lui/leur) or tonique (à lui/à elle — Cluster 9).

> ❌ *Je pense à Marie* → *J'y pense* (incorrect — Marie is a person)
> ✅ *Je pense à Marie* → *Je pense à elle* (tonique, see Cluster 9)

### Y replaces "place" markers in general

> *Tu vas chez le dentiste ?* → *Tu y vas ?*
> *Il rentre dans sa chambre.* → *Il y rentre.*
> *Nous travaillons sur ce projet.* → *Nous y travaillons.*

Anywhere there's a place or destination introduced by a preposition (à, chez, dans, sur, en — when "place"), Y handles the substitution.

## What EN replaces

### EN replaces "de + thing"

> *Tu parles de ton voyage ?* → *Tu en parles ?*
> *Elle s'occupe de ses enfants.* → *Elle s'en occupe.*
> *Nous discutons de la situation.* → *Nous en discutons.*

Like Y for "à + person," EN does NOT replace "de + person."

> ❌ *Je parle de Marc* → *J'en parle* (incorrect — Marc is a person)
> ✅ *Je parle de Marc* → *Je parle de lui* (tonique)

### EN replaces partitive (du, de la, des, de l') and quantities

> *Tu veux du café ?* → *Tu en veux ?*
> *Elle a des amis ici.* → *Elle en a.*
> *Nous mangeons trois pommes.* → *Nous en mangeons trois.* (note: number stays)
> *J'ai beaucoup de travail.* → *J'en ai beaucoup.*
> *Tu n'as pas de problème.* → *Tu n'en as pas.*

This is the partitive use, and it's where EN appears most often in everyday speech.

## Placement: same as COD/COI

> *J'y vais.* / *J'y suis allé.* / *Je vais y aller.* / *Je n'y vais pas.*
> *J'en veux.* / *J'en ai voulu.* / *Je vais en vouloir.* / *Je n'en veux pas.*

Same rules. Same automation challenge.

- **Marker B1.2.C7.b — "EN avoidance":** EN usage rate <0.20 in contexts where EN was applicable

# CLUSTER 8 — Double Pronouns: The Combinatorial Layer

```
Cluster ID:           B1.2-C8
Grammar topic:        Combining two pronouns in a single sentence (me le, te la, lui en, le leur, etc.)
Vocabulary theme:     Les transactions / Donner et recevoir
Tâche application:    Tâche 2 (interactive exchanges with implicit transactions)
Format:               In-app markdown
Estimated time:       45-60 minutes
Difficulty:           Advanced — but unavoidable for B2 fluency
Prerequisites:        Clusters 5, 6, 7 all at status Absorbed or Partial
```

## Why this cluster matters

Once you can use single pronouns, you'll quickly hit sentences that need two:

> *Je donne le livre à Marie.* → *Je le lui donne.* (le = the book; lui = to Marie)
> *Tu m'as parlé de cette idée.* → *Tu m'en as parlé.* (m' = to me; en = of this idea)

Anglophones avoid this construction with even more determination than they avoid Y/EN. The result is a B1 ceiling that's almost impossible to break without explicit practice: students keep producing one-pronoun sentences with explicit nouns elsewhere.

## The order rule (the only complicated part)

Two pronouns in a sentence stack in a fixed order. The order depends on which pronouns combine:

### Rule: When the pronouns are at different "levels"

| 1st position | 2nd position |
|---|---|
| me, te, se, nous, vous | le, la, les |

> *Tu me donnes le livre.* → *Tu me le donnes.* (me before le)
> *Elle nous montre la photo.* → *Elle nous la montre.*

### Rule: When both pronouns are 3rd person

| 1st position | 2nd position |
|---|---|
| le, la, les | lui, leur |

> *Je donne le livre à Marie.* → *Je le lui donne.* (le before lui)
> *Nous expliquons la situation aux enfants.* → *Nous la leur expliquons.*

### Rule: Y and EN go last

| Final position |
|---|
| y, en |

> *Tu me donnes du café ?* → *Tu m'en donnes ?* (m' before en)
> *Il nous emmène à la plage.* → *Il nous y emmène.*

When y and en appear together (rare), the order is **y en** (mnemonic: "Il y en a" — there is some).

> *Il y a des problèmes.* → *Il y en a.*

## The shortcut: a clean ordering chart

Memorize this single ordering for double pronouns before the verb:

```
me                  le               lui              y           en
te                  la               leur
se     +            les       +                 +              +
nous
vous
```

You pick one from each column (only those needed). Skip columns you don't use. The order is left-to-right, always.

Examples:
- *me + le* → *me le*
- *le + lui* → *le lui*
- *te + en* → *t'en*
- *me + y* → *m'y*
- *le + y* → *l'y* (rare but correct)

## Placement: same as before

Pronouns stack BEFORE the verb (or auxiliary) in simple/compound tenses, and BEFORE the infinitive when applicable.

> *Je le lui donne.* / *Je le lui ai donné.* / *Je vais le lui donner.* / *Je ne le lui donne pas.*

# CLUSTER 9 — Pronoms toniques: Emphasis and "After Preposition" Cases

```
Cluster ID:           B1.2-C9
Grammar topic:        Pronoms toniques (moi, toi, lui, elle, nous, vous, eux, elles)
Vocabulary theme:     L'identité / L'opinion personnelle
Tâche application:    Tâche 3 (expressing opinions, contrast, emphasis)
Format:               In-app markdown
Estimated time:       45-60 minutes
Difficulty:           Intermediate — fixes a specific class of errors students keep making in clusters 5-8
Prerequisites:        Clusters 5-8 (the unstressed pronoun system)
```

## Why this cluster fixes errors from earlier clusters

Toniques are the corrective. They appear in five specific contexts where the unstressed pronouns from clusters 5-8 don't work. Anglophones who learned only the unstressed forms produce errors in exactly these five contexts.

## The five contexts where toniques fire

### Context 1: After a preposition

> *Je travaille avec **lui**.* (NOT *Je travaille avec il*)
> *C'est pour **moi** ou pour **toi** ?*
> *Sans **eux**, on n'aurait pas réussi.*
> *Je pense à **elle**.* (people, not things — fixes the Y trap from Cluster 7)

### Context 2: For emphasis or contrast

> ***Moi***, je préfère le café. **Toi**, tu préfères le thé.
> *C'est **lui** qui a tort.* (not "c'est il...")
> *Eux, ils ne comprennent pas.*

### Context 3: After "c'est" / "ce sont"

> *C'est **moi**.* / *C'est **lui**.* / *Ce sont **eux**.*

NOT *C'est je* / *C'est il*. The tonique is mandatory after c'est.

### Context 4: With "et" / "ni" connecting subjects

> ***Lui** et **moi**, nous avons décidé.* (or *Toi et moi, nous avons décidé.*)
> *Ni **lui** ni **elle** ne savent.*

### Context 5: Standalone responses

> *— Qui a fait ça ? — **Moi**.* (NOT *Je*)
> *— Qui veut du café ? — **Lui** !*

## The form

| Subject pronoun | Tonique |
|---|---|
| je | moi |
| tu | toi |
| il | lui |
| elle | elle |
| on | nous (or soi for indefinite) |
| nous | nous |
| vous | vous |
| ils | eux |
| elles | elles |

Note: *elle*, *nous*, *vous*, *elles* are identical to their unstressed forms. The differences are *moi/toi/lui/eux* — these are the four to memorize.

## Phase B1.2 — Synthesis

By the end of this phase, the student should be able to:

1. Replace any direct object with COD, in any tense, with correct placement
2. Distinguish COD-verbs from COI-verbs and use the correct pronoun automatically
3. Use Y for places/things-with-à and EN for things-with-de + partitives + quantities
4. Combine pronouns in correct order (le lui, m'en, vous y) without effort
5. Reach for toniques in the five contexts that require them

The combined effect: a B1 student who entered this phase saying *J'ai vu Marie hier. J'ai parlé à Marie de mon projet* now says *Je l'ai vue hier. Je lui en ai parlé.* Two sentences, four pronouns, and the production sounds B2.

This is not stylistic — it's structural. The sentence is shorter, more naturally weighted, and the speaker sounds fluent rather than translating word-by-word.

## Spiral connection

Phase B1.2 sets up:
- **Cluster B1.4-C16 (Les Moules)** — pronoun automation is one of the anti-translation reflexes Les Moules locks down
- **Cluster B2.1 territory** — once pronouns are automatic, advanced register variations (mise en relief, c'est…que constructions) become accessible
- **Cluster B1.5-C19 (Concordance des temps)** — reported speech requires fluid pronoun handling

## Authoring notes

1. **Why this phase compresses 5 clusters into one document:** the five clusters are a single grammatical system. Treating them as five separate weeks creates artificial isolation. By document-level treatment, the student sees how the pieces interact (especially Cluster 8's combinations and Cluster 9's corrections to 5-7).

2. **Speed-drill emphasis:** unlike Phase B1.1 which was tense-system-heavy, this phase emphasizes production speed (Exercises 5.2, 8.3). Pronoun fluency is a speed problem, not a knowledge problem.

3. **L1 traps named per cluster:** each cluster names the specific anglophone error. The verb-COI list in Cluster 6 (téléphoner à, plaire à, manquer à, etc.) is gold — these errors fire in 80%+ of anglophone B1 production.

4. **What's NOT here:** reflexive pronouns (se laver, etc.) are deferred to Phase B1.3 or earlier review. They're a separate sub-system. Inserting them here would dilute focus.

5. **What review should focus on:**
   - Is the verb-COI list complete? You've heard real anglophone errors — are there trap-verbs I missed?
   - Are the speed thresholds (under 2 seconds, under 3 seconds) realistic for B1+ students? May need calibration.
   - Is the pronoun ordering chart in Cluster 8 the cleanest presentation? Or should it be a different visualization?
   - Should Cluster 9 (toniques) come before or after Cluster 8 (doubles)? I sequenced toniques last because they're a corrective layer, but the alternative (toniques before doubles, since they're prerequisite for prep contexts) is defensible.

6. **Recommended next document:** Phase B1.3 (Clusters 10-13) — pronoms relatifs, intermediate connectors, discours indirect au présent, expression de la cause. This is the subordination phase.
