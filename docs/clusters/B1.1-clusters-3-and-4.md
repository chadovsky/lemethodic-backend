# B1.1 — Phase 1 Clusters 3 & 4: The Future + Conditional System

**Path:** B1 → B2 (Visa-Urgent / TCF Canada)
**Phase:** B1.1 — Tense Mastery (concluding pair)
**Clusters in this document:** Cluster 3 (Futur simple + Futur antérieur) + Cluster 4 (Conditionnel présent — introduction)
**Format:** In-app markdown lesson + structured exercises + Tâche-shaped practice prompt + detection rubric
**Author:** Chadi Bakhay (drafted by Co-PM Claude)
**Status:** Draft v0.1
**Prerequisites:** Clusters B1.1-C1 and B1.1-C2 (Imparfait/PC/PQP) at status Absorbed or Partial

---

## Why this pair completes Phase B1.1

Clusters 1 and 2 gave the student a complete past system. This pair gives them a complete forward-looking system:

- **Cluster 3 (Futur)** = projecting forward from the present, sequencing future events, predicting outcomes, making promises and plans
- **Cluster 4 (Conditionnel)** = softening, hypothesizing, expressing politeness, imagining alternatives — the social register a B1 student urgently needs but rarely uses

Together with Clusters 1-2, the student now has 5 tense reference points (PC, imparfait, PQP, futur simple, futur antérieur) plus the conditionnel mode. That's enough scaffolding for any TCF Tâche 1, 2, or 3 prompt that doesn't require subjonctif — which is most of them.

This pair also closes the door on a major B1 ceiling marker: students who narrate the past well but collapse into present tense the moment they need to talk about future or hypothetical. Examiners notice this collapse immediately.

# CLUSTER 3 — Futur simple + Futur antérieur: Projecting Forward

```
Cluster ID:           B1.1-C3
Grammar topic:        Futur simple (the simple future) and Futur antérieur (the future perfect)
Vocabulary theme:     Mes projets / Mon avenir au Canada
Tâche application:    Tâche 1 + Tâche 3 — projection, planning, prediction
Format:               In-app markdown
Estimated time:       75-90 minutes
Difficulty:           Intermediate — futur simple is structurally easy, futur antérieur is the B2-revealing layer
```

## Why most students fail this in production

Two failure modes, both common:

**Failure mode 1: Defaulting to futur proche.**
Most A2/B1 students learn `aller + infinitif` first (`je vais partir`, `nous allons travailler`). It's easier, sounds natural in casual speech, and works most of the time. So they keep using it. Forever.

The result: a student who at B1+ should be using `je partirai`, `nous travaillerons`, `il fera beau` is still saying `je vais partir`, `nous allons travailler`, `il va faire beau` for everything — including formal contexts, predictions, hypothetical chains, and TCF Tâche 3 monologues where futur simple signals a more controlled register.

**Failure mode 2: Never using futur antérieur.**
Even students who use futur simple comfortably almost never reach for `j'aurai fini`, `il sera parti`, `nous aurons compris`. This is the tense that says "by then, X will have happened" — and B1 students don't have it in their active production. Result: timeline collapse in the future direction, exactly as PQP-absence collapses the past direction.

## The L1 trap: English future is messier than French future

English has multiple ways to mark future:
- `will + verb` (I will work)
- `going to + verb` (I am going to work)
- `present continuous` (I am working tomorrow)
- `present simple` (I work tomorrow at 8)
- `shall + verb` (formal/British)

French is simpler and more rigid. Two main forms:
- `futur proche` (`aller + infinitif`) — for plans, intentions, near future, casual speech
- `futur simple` (one-word conjugation) — for predictions, formal statements, statements with future-time markers, hypothetical chains

The trap: anglophones translate ALL their varied future forms into `futur proche` because it feels closest to "going to" — which in English itself has expanded to cover almost any future use. This collapses register and makes production sound undifferentiated.

### The register distinction (the part nobody teaches)

| Context | Use this in French |
|---|---|
| Informal chat, immediate plans | Futur proche: *Je vais aller au cinéma ce soir.* |
| Formal speech, predictions, TCF monologue | Futur simple: *J'irai au cinéma ce soir.* |
| After "quand", "lorsque", "dès que" referring to future | Futur simple (mandatory): *Quand j'aurai du temps, je t'appellerai.* |
| Hypothetical "if X, then Y" (real future) | Futur simple in the result: *Si je peux, je viendrai.* |
| What will have been done before another future moment | Futur antérieur: *Quand tu arriveras, j'aurai déjà fini.* |

### The mandatory futur after temporal conjunctions (the most common B1 error)

In English: *When I arrive, I will call you.* — present + future.
In French: *Quand j'arriverai, je t'appellerai.* — **future + future**, both clauses.

English uses present after "when" referring to future. French uses future. This is a structural divergence anglophone B1 students miss systematically.

> ❌ *Quand je suis prêt, je commencerai.*
> ✅ **Quand je serai prêt, je commencerai.**

Same for `lorsque`, `dès que`, `aussitôt que`, `tant que`. All require futur (or futur antérieur) in both clauses when the meaning is future.

## The mental model: futur simple vs futur antérieur

Think of these two tenses as the future-direction mirror of PC/PQP:

| Past direction | Future direction |
|---|---|
| **Imparfait** = background in the past | (no equivalent — French has no "future imparfait") |
| **Passé composé** = main events in the past | **Futur simple** = main events in the future |
| **Plus-que-parfait** = before a past reference point | **Futur antérieur** = before a future reference point |

The futur antérieur uses `avoir`/`être` in futur simple + past participle:
- *J'aurai fini* (I will have finished)
- *Tu seras parti* (you will have left)
- *Nous aurons compris* (we will have understood)

Same auxiliary rules as PC and PQP. If you can produce `j'ai fini` and `j'avais fini`, you can produce `j'aurai fini` — same logic, different auxiliary tense.

## When futur antérieur fires

Three triggers, all anchored to a future reference point:

### Trigger 1: explicit "by then" + completed action

> **D'ici la fin de l'année, j'aurai économisé assez pour le voyage.**
> *By the end of the year, I will have saved enough for the trip.*

Markers: `d'ici X`, `avant X`, `à la fin de`, `dans Y mois/ans`.

### Trigger 2: sequencing two future events with "quand/dès que/lorsque"

> **Dès que j'aurai reçu la confirmation, je te préviendrai.**
> *As soon as I receive the confirmation, I'll let you know.*

Notice the chronology: two future events, where one must happen before the other. The earlier one goes in futur antérieur, the later one in futur simple. **English uses present + future. French uses futur antérieur + futur simple.**

This is one of the most B2-revealing structures. A student who handles this fluently is operating above B1.

### Trigger 3: predicting completion

> **Dans dix ans, l'intelligence artificielle aura transformé le marché du travail.**
> *In ten years, AI will have transformed the job market.*

Used in opinion essays, predictions, TCF Tâche 3 monologues on social/technological topics. Fires often once you're aware of it.

## The practical anglophone progression

If you're stuck in `aller + infinitif` for everything:

1. **First**: learn to use futur simple in formal contexts and after `quand`/`lorsque`/`dès que` when the meaning is future. This single rule fixes 60% of the production gap.
2. **Second**: learn to recognize "by-then" markers and reach for futur antérieur. Adds the B2-revealing layer.
3. **Third**: keep `aller + infinitif` for genuine near-future plans and informal speech. Don't kill it — calibrate it.

## Practice Prompt (Tâche 3 — Monologue argumentatif, 5 min recording)

> **« Décrivez vos projets pour les cinq prochaines années. Expliquez quels seront vos objectifs principaux, comment vous comptez les atteindre, et ce que vous aurez accompli au bout de cette période. Soyez précis sur les étapes et les obstacles potentiels. »**
>
> Vous avez 1 minute pour réfléchir, puis 5 minutes pour répondre.

This prompt is engineered to force a tense-rich projection:
- **Vos objectifs** → futur simple (ce que vous ferez)
- **Comment vous comptez les atteindre** → mix of futur simple and conditional-style softening (Cluster 4 territory)
- **Ce que vous aurez accompli au bout** → futur antérieur (the B2-revealing layer)
- **Étapes et obstacles** → conditional/hypothetical phrasing creeps in naturally

Failure modes:
1. **Futur proche flood** — "je vais faire, je vais aller, je vais étudier" — collapses register, reads A2-B1
2. **No futur antérieur** — only main events, no "by then" layer — flat future timeline

## Detection Rubric (engine-facing)

### Quantitative signals

| Signal | Computation | Threshold |
|---|---|---|
| Futur proche ratio | `count(futur_proche) / count(future_verbs_total)` | Healthy: <0.50 in formal Tâche prompts. >0.80 = "futur proche flood" |
| Futur simple presence | `count(futur_simple)` in projection narrative >60s | 0 = under-use flag (formal context) |
| Futur antérieur presence | `count(futur_anterieur)` in 3-min projection | 0 = under-use flag (B2 ceiling marker) |
| Quand/dès que/lorsque + present | Detection of temporal conjunction followed by present-tense verb in future-meaning context | Any occurrence = "temporal conjunction error" |
| Irregular futur stems errors | Detection of regular endings on irregular stems (e.g., `je sairai` instead of `je serai`) | Per-occurrence flag |

### Qualitative markers (B1 ceiling, fires when...)

- **Marker B1.1.C3.a — "Futur proche flood":** futur proche ratio >0.80 in a formal Tâche prompt
- **Marker B1.1.C3.b — "FA absence":** zero futur antérieur in a 3-min projection narrative containing future temporal anchors
- **Marker B1.1.C3.c — "Temporal conjunction transfer":** present tense after `quand/lorsque/dès que` in a future-meaning context
- **Marker B1.1.C3.d — "Irregular stem failure":** ≥3 irregular-stem errors in a recording
- **Marker B1.1.C3.e — "Future avoidance":** total future-tense verbs <5 in a 3-min projection narrative — student is dodging the tense entirely

### Cluster status assignment

| Status | Conditions |
|---|---|
| **Absorbed** | Markers C3.a–e all silent. At least 1 futur antérieur in projection. Temporal conjunctions handled correctly. |
| **Partial** | At most 1 marker firing. Futur simple present, futur antérieur sporadic. |
| **Needs revisit** | 2+ markers firing OR marker C3.b alone (FA absence) OR marker C3.c alone (temporal conjunction transfer is structural). |
| **Not started** | No future-tense production yet. |

The prescription engine routes "needs revisit" students back to Exercises 3.3 (temporal conjunction trap) and 3.4 (FA trigger drill) before re-attempting the practice prompt.

# CLUSTER 4 — Conditionnel présent: Softening, Hypothesizing, Imagining

```
Cluster ID:           B1.1-C4
Grammar topic:        Conditionnel présent — introduction (politeness, hypothetical, softening)
Vocabulary theme:     Si je pouvais... / Mes rêves et mes hésitations
Tâche application:    Tâche 1 — politeness register; Tâche 3 — hypothetical reasoning
Format:               In-app markdown
Estimated time:       60-75 minutes
Difficulty:           Intermediate — form is easy, usage reveals B1 vs B2
Prerequisites:        Cluster B1.1-C3 (Futur simple — same stem)
```

## Why this cluster matters more than students realize

A B1 student who lacks conditionnel sounds **brusque** and **inflexible** in French — even if their grammar is otherwise solid. The conditionnel is the social lubricant of French communication. Without it:

- Requests sound demanding: ❌ *Je veux un café* instead of ✅ *Je voudrais un café*
- Suggestions sound flat: ❌ *Tu peux m'aider ?* instead of ✅ *Tu pourrais m'aider ?*
- Hypothetical reasoning is impossible: ❌ *Si j'ai de l'argent, je voyage* (incoherent) instead of ✅ *Si j'avais de l'argent, je voyagerais*
- Reported speech in past collapses (Cluster B1.3-C12 territory)

In TCF Canada specifically, Tâche 1 (the role-play) heavily rewards politeness register. A candidate who uses conditionnel correctly in their requests sounds adult, professional, and culturally competent. A candidate who doesn't sounds like a tourist with a phrasebook.

This cluster is the **social register key**.

## The L1 trap: English politeness is lexical, French politeness is grammatical

English signals politeness through word choice and tone:
- "Could you...?" / "Would you mind...?" / "I was wondering if..."
- These are vocabulary changes, not tense changes.

French signals politeness through tense choice:
- *Pouvez-vous...?* (present, neutral) → *Pourriez-vous...?* (conditionnel, polite)
- *Je veux...* (present, blunt) → *Je voudrais...* (conditionnel, polite)
- *Tu as une minute ?* (present, casual) → *Tu aurais une minute ?* (conditionnel, softer)

Anglophone B1 students who learned vocabulary politeness in English try to import the same strategy — adding *s'il vous plaît* to everything, or stacking modifiers like *peut-être* and *si possible*. These don't replace the conditionnel. They complement it. **The tense itself does the politeness work in French.**

## The mental model: the conditionnel does four things

Don't memorize "conditionnel = would". The mapping is too noisy. Memorize the four uses:

### Use 1: Politeness (the most frequent use, the one anglophones miss most)

> **Je voudrais un renseignement, s'il vous plaît.**
> *I would like some information, please.*

> **Pourriez-vous me dire où se trouve la station ?**
> *Could you tell me where the station is?*

> **Auriez-vous deux minutes pour discuter ?**
> *Would you have two minutes to talk?*

Verbs that go conditionnel for politeness: `vouloir, pouvoir, aimer, savoir, avoir, devoir`. This list covers ~90% of polite requests.

### Use 2: Hypothetical with si + imparfait

> **Si j'avais le temps, je voyagerais plus.**
> *If I had the time, I would travel more.*

The structure: **Si + imparfait → conditionnel**. This is the "imagined alternative" pattern, where the if-condition is unreal or unlikely.

Compare with the "real future" pattern from Cluster 3:
- *Si j'ai le temps, je voyagerai* (real condition — present + futur simple)
- *Si j'avais le temps, je voyagerais* (hypothetical — imparfait + conditionnel)

Anglophones often collapse these two patterns because English uses "would" in both. **French distinguishes by tense.**

### Use 3: Softening assertions and giving advice

> **Tu devrais essayer.**
> *You should try.*

> **Ça pourrait marcher.**
> *That could work.*

> **Il vaudrait mieux partir tôt.**
> *It would be better to leave early.*

`devrais, pourrais, faudrait, vaudrait` — these are the advice/suggestion forms. Without them, advice in French sounds like commands.

### Use 4: Hearsay / reported information

> **D'après les médias, le ministre serait à Paris.**
> *According to the media, the minister is reportedly in Paris.*

> **Le suspect aurait déjà quitté le pays.**
> *The suspect has reportedly already left the country.*

This is a journalistic register. B1 students don't need to produce this actively, but they should **recognize** it. Most TCF reading-comprehension texts use it.

## The form: futur stem + imparfait endings

If you can do futur simple, you already know the conditionnel stem.

| Infinitif | Futur simple stem | Conditionnel (je) |
|---|---|---|
| parler | parler- | je parlerais |
| finir | finir- | je finirais |
| aller | ir- | j'irais |
| être | ser- | je serais |
| avoir | aur- | j'aurais |
| faire | fer- | je ferais |
| pouvoir | pourr- | je pourrais |
| vouloir | voudr- | je voudrais |
| savoir | saur- | je saurais |
| devoir | devr- | je devrais |

Endings: -ais, -ais, -ait, -ions, -iez, -aient (same as imparfait).

The form is borderline trivial once futur simple is locked. **The cluster is about usage, not conjugation.**

## A practical anglophone progression

If you've never used conditionnel actively:

1. **First**: replace *Je veux* with *Je voudrais* in every request. Replace *Tu peux* with *Tu pourrais*. This single substitution unlocks 70% of politeness register.
2. **Second**: practice the **Si + imparfait → conditionnel** structure. Build hypothetical sentences about your own life: *Si j'habitais à Montréal, je...*
3. **Third**: use *tu devrais / vous devriez* when giving advice. Stop saying *tu dois* and *vous devez* unless you mean an obligation.
4. **Fourth (recognition only)**: notice the hearsay use in news headlines and TCF reading texts.

## Detection Rubric (engine-facing)

### Quantitative signals

| Signal | Computation | Threshold |
|---|---|---|
| Conditionnel presence | `count(conditionnel_verbs)` in 5-min recording | 0 = severe under-use flag |
| Politeness conditionnel ratio | `count(je_voudrais + pourriez_vous + auriez_vous + tu_pourrais...)` in request contexts | 0 in a request prompt = "politeness register failure" |
| Si + imparfait → conditionnel chain | Detection of "si X imparfait, Y conditionnel" structure | Any correct occurrence = healthy. None in hypothetical prompt = under-use. |
| Conditionnel for confident assertion | `je saurais` or `j'aurais` in clear knowledge-assertion context | Possible over-use flag |
| Mixed if-clause structure | "Si j'ai... je voyagerais" (present in si + conditionnel in result) | Any occurrence = "if-clause structural error" |

### Qualitative markers (B1 ceiling, fires when...)

- **Marker B1.1.C4.a — "Politeness register failure":** zero conditionnel uses in a request/Tâche 1 prompt
- **Marker B1.1.C4.b — "Hypothetical structural error":** mixing tenses in si-clause (e.g., `Si + present + conditionnel` instead of `Si + imparfait + conditionnel`)
- **Marker B1.1.C4.c — "Conditionnel avoidance":** zero conditionnel verbs in a hypothetical prompt
- **Marker B1.1.C4.d — "Conditionnel over-use":** conditionnel applied to non-politeness, non-hypothetical contexts
- **Marker B1.1.C4.e — "Politesse lexicale only":** student adds `s'il vous plaît / si possible / peut-être` to every request without using conditionnel — anglophone politeness-strategy transfer
