## The economic backdrop your buyer actually lives in

Before defensibility, **let's establish what the TCF candidate actually has at stake.** This reframes everything downstream.

### The Canadian PR economics

A TCF Canada candidate isn't shopping for "language learning." They're shopping for a **CRS score boost** that gets them an Invitation to Apply (ITA) for Canadian Permanent Residency.

Here are the real numbers, based on official IRCC data and 2025 draws:

- **TCF Canada exam fee:** $390 CAD (one-time, retake-able after 30 days)
- **NCLC 7 in French (B2 level):** Worth **+50 CRS points** if English ≥ CLB 5
- **NCLC 7 in French:** Worth **+25 CRS points** even if English < CLB 4
- **Total potential CRS gain:** Up to 62 points (50 from "Additional Factors" + 12 from "Core Human Capital")
- **March 21, 2025 French-language draw cutoff:** 379 CRS — vs general draw cutoff of 549 CRS
- **Total Express Entry application cost:** ~$1,500-2,500 in fees (PR fee, language test, ECA, biometrics, medical)
- **Total relocation cost incl. PoF:** $13,000-30,000+ for sole applicant

### What this means for pricing tolerance

A TCF candidate going for Canadian PR is mid-investment in a **$15,000-30,000 immigration journey**. The TCF score is the single most important variable in that journey for French-proficient candidates — it can be the difference between an ITA in 6 months and waiting indefinitely.

**Their willingness to pay for prep is anchored to outcome value, not per-month subscription cost.** A $500 prep package that meaningfully improves their TCF score is rational. A $30/month subscription that helps them pass is rational.

Compare to a Promova user learning French casually for travel. **Different buyer entirely.** That buyer is price-sensitive; the TCF candidate is outcome-sensitive.

### The Indian / Vietnamese / Brazilian / Arab buyer profile

You called out your real audience: TCF Canada candidates from India, China, Brazil, Middle East/Arabic-speaking countries. Some Europeans.

What this audience has in common:
- **High motivation.** They're not learning French for fun. They're solving a specific problem (PR, CRS points, family reunification, work permit).
- **Limited French exposure.** They're often working from a low base (A0-A1) and need to hit B2 (NCLC 7) — that's 600-800 hours of structured study.
- **Income disparity matters.** Indian buyer purchasing power ≠ French buyer purchasing power. $15-30/month is meaningful for an Indian PR applicant.
- **English-comfortable.** They learn through English. Your platform's bilingual support (English instructions + French content) is structurally aligned.
- **Tutor-cautious.** Many have been burned by unstructured tutors. They want curriculum, not just chat.

This is a higher-conversion, higher-LTV buyer than the general Promova/Duolingo audience. **Recognize this in pricing.**

## The five defensibility questions

### Q1: Why would someone pay [BRAND] $X over PrepMyFuture's $39?

**Confidence: high.** This is your most important price-anchor question.

PrepMyFuture's $39 buys:
- 40 study sheets (text)
- 3 full practice tests (text MCQ)
- 1,000+ exercises (text MCQ)
- 12-month access
- Detailed answer explanations
- Bilingual (FR/EN)

What it explicitly does NOT buy:
- **No oral expression practice.** You read this as "they ignore the speaking section." Verified.
- **No video lessons.** It's text-based.
- **No personalized curriculum.** Same content for everyone.
- **No AI feedback on student-produced output.**
- **No mobile app.** Web only.
- **No spaced repetition or memory science.**
- **No diagnostic-driven path.** Linear study sheets.

**Your defensibility argument against PrepMyFuture:**

Two-part:

**Part A — Different value buckets, not direct comparison.**
PrepMyFuture sells "review materials." [BRAND] sells "training program." Different categories. A serious TCF candidate uses BOTH:
- PrepMyFuture for: practice MCQ, exam familiarity, vocab review
- [BRAND] for: speaking practice, oral expression coaching, curriculum, feedback, score guarantee

This isn't a "buy one or the other" decision. It's "buy both because they serve different needs." Position [BRAND] as **complement to PrepMyFuture, not replacement.** This dissolves the price comparison.

**Part B — The expression orale gap is huge.**
The TCF Canada has 4 sections:
1. Listening comprehension (35 min, MCQ)
2. Reading comprehension (60 min, MCQ)
3. Written expression (60 min, 3 exercises)
4. **Oral expression (12 min, individual face-to-face with examiner — 3 tasks)**

PrepMyFuture prepares 1, 2, 3 well. They essentially ignore section 4. **Section 4 is what makes or breaks the NCLC 7 score for many candidates** — it's the only section that requires real-time human-like fluency.

[BRAND]'s entire reason for existing is to fix this gap. Your pitch:

> "PrepMyFuture for the MCQ sections. [BRAND] for the speaking section. Together, you're prepared."

Price implication: you can charge $19-29/month or $99-149 one-time and not be in price competition with PrepMyFuture at all. They prep different things.

### Q2: Why would someone pay [BRAND] $X over FluentLab's $0?

**Confidence: medium-high.** This is your hardest emotional question. Let's address it concretely.

FluentLab.org is **for IELTS, not TCF.** A TCF candidate physically cannot use FluentLab to prep for their actual exam. The framing "but FluentLab is free" implicitly assumes the buyer can substitute one for the other. They cannot.

But the spirit of your concern is real: **what if a FluentLab equivalent appears for TCF tomorrow?** Let's address that directly.

**Three structural moats against generic AI-feedback competitors:**

**Moat 1 — Language pedagogy depth, specifically L1 interference patterns.**

FluentLab's AI scores generic IELTS dimensions: pronunciation, fluency, grammar, vocabulary, coherence. It doesn't know that:
- Anglophones systematically use `there is + Y for X time` instead of `il y a Y pour X temps`
- Indian speakers transfer `make a question` from Hindi grammar
- Vietnamese speakers struggle with French liaison patterns
- Arabic speakers conflate `de` and `à` because Arabic doesn't distinguish source/destination prepositions the same way

**Generic AI feedback doesn't catch L1-specific errors.** Your Les Moules framework is built precisely for this. After 7,000 hours, you have a structured taxonomy of errors-by-source-language. That's a moat that doesn't disappear just because someone clones the surface AI.

**Moat 2 — Curriculum depth, specifically the Tâche 1/2/3 mapping.**

FluentLab does generic IELTS speaking parts. It doesn't have:
- Pre-built argument matrices for 20 common TCF Tâche 3 themes
- Modular plug-and-play templates for opinion expression
- Thematic grouping tables that students can use during the 4.5-minute Tâche 3 prep
- A 16-lesson L'École path designed around the actual TCF skills hierarchy

**Curriculum is not commoditized.** AI doesn't generate good curriculum unaided. Your existing tutoring documents are training data competitors can't replicate.

**Moat 3 — The teacher behind the product.**

This is the moat you keep undervaluing. Three reinforcing mechanisms:

1. **Trust transfer.** A student who watches Chadi explain L1 interference in a 3-min video trusts Chadi the teacher. They'll trust Chadi's product. They won't trust a faceless AI app the same way.

2. **Living content.** Every week you can record a new video answering a real student question. FluentLab can't do this — they have no human face. Your content becomes a moving target competitors can't catch.

3. **Sales channels.** You can do TikTok in French and Arabic. You can sell to Moroccans, Algerians, Tunisians, Indians (you have Preply data on this), Vietnamese (high TCF Canada applicant volume), Brazilians (your stated audience). **A faceless AI app can't have a YouTube channel that converts.** You can.

**Pricing implication.** Even if a free FluentLab-for-French appears tomorrow, [BRAND] is differentiated on:
- Pedagogy specificity (L1 interference)
- Curriculum depth
- Human-led trust + content + sales

That justifies a $14.99-29.99/month subscription premium against free competitors **provided you make these moats visible in marketing.** If you sell yourself as "AI feedback on speaking," you'll lose the comparison. If you sell yourself as "Chadi's TCF method, scaled," you won't.

**The buyer isn't choosing between Promova and [BRAND]. They're potentially using BOTH.** Promova for general French in spare time. [BRAND] for exam-focused training in the 3-6 months leading up to the exam date.

**Pricing implication.** Promova at $14/mo is not a price ceiling for [BRAND]. The relevant comparison is to Magoosh ($99-$199 for 1-6 months) and Kaplan ($449-$2,000) — exam-prep platforms in adjacent verticals. Those validate that exam prep commands 3-10x the price of general language learning.

### Q4: Why would someone pay [BRAND] $X over a Preply tutor at $25/hour?

**Confidence: medium-high.** This one needs honest math.

Reality: a serious TCF candidate often DOES hire a tutor. Many of your past Preply students are exactly this profile. You're not just competing with their wallet — you're competing with the **alternative use of their wallet.**

**Honest scenario: 3 months of TCF prep.**

Tutor-only path:
- 3 sessions/week × 12 weeks = 36 sessions × $25/hr = **$900**
- Pros: Personalized, adaptive, real human accountability
- Cons: Expensive, scheduling-dependent, only when tutor is available, depends on tutor quality

[BRAND] only path (at $19/month):
- $19 × 3 = **$57** total
- Pros: Cheap, 24/7, structured curriculum, AI feedback
- Cons: No human, no accountability, no "did I really get this right?" moments

[BRAND] + tutor hybrid (at $19/month + 1 session/week):
- $57 + 12 × $25 = **$357 total**
- Pros: 75% cheaper than tutor-only, retains human accountability for hard moments, AI handles bulk practice
- Cons: Still requires scheduling 1 session/week

**The pitch is not "[BRAND] replaces tutor." It's "[BRAND] makes your tutoring 75% cheaper without losing quality."**

This is the actual selling proposition. A Preply student paying $300/month for tutoring can switch to $19/month [BRAND] + 1 monthly tutor session at $25 = $44/month. They save $250/month with comparable outcomes. That's a real value proposition.

**Strategic implication.** You can SELL TO PREPLY TUTORS, not just compete with them.

Imagine a "[BRAND] for Tutors" tier:
- $30/month per tutor
- Tutor invites their 5-10 students to [BRAND] platform
- Students get structured practice between sessions
- Tutor gets dashboard showing what each student practiced, where they struggle, what to focus on next session
- Tutor session quality goes up because they're not wasting time on "show me what you've been working on"
- Tutor retention goes up because students see better results

This is the **Position C "tutor-in-a-box"** model from Block 1. Genuinely strategic. Worth a real conversation in Block 4.

### Q5: Why would someone pay [BRAND] $X over doing nothing / YouTube self-study?

**Confidence: high.** Often the most underestimated competitor.

The TCF candidate's actual default behavior:
1. Watch some YouTube TCF prep videos (free)
2. Download some PDFs of past papers (free, often pirated)
3. Practice with a friend who speaks French (free)
4. Take the TCF exam ($390)
5. Possibly fail to hit NCLC 7
6. Pay $390 to retake
7. Repeat

**This is the silent majority of the market.** Maybe 60-70% of TCF candidates self-study from free resources.

**Your pitch to this segment:**
"You can spend 600-800 hours self-studying with random YouTube videos and hope for NCLC 7. Or you can spend $19/month for 3 months ($57 total) on a structured program built specifically for the TCF, and dramatically increase your odds of hitting NCLC 7 on the first try. Cost of one failed retake: $390. Cost of [BRAND]: $57. Math is clear."

**Score guarantee makes this even sharper.** "If you complete the program and don't improve, money back." Magoosh does this and it works.

## The "score guarantee" question

You should adopt a **TCF score guarantee** as a marketing weapon. Concrete proposal:

> "Complete the L'École path (16 lessons + 5 mock exams over 8 weeks). If you don't improve at least 1 NCLC level on a follow-up TCF mock, full refund."

Three things this does:
- Massively reduces purchase friction
- Forces buyers to actually use the product (refund requires program completion)
- Self-selects motivated students (low refund rate among completers)

Magoosh has run this for 15+ years. It works. Refund rates are in the 2-5% range when paired with completion requirements.

## Pricing recommendation — three scenarios

Based on the above, three viable pricing models. **Confidence: medium-high on which works best.**

### Scenario A — One-time pricing (PrepMyFuture-shaped)

- $79 for 6 months access
- $129 for 12 months access
- Single payment, simple to understand
- Direct comparison to PrepMyFuture's $39-99 framing

**Pros:** Lower friction, no churn, easy to market.
**Cons:** Lower LTV per user. Can't grow into platform model. Limits Phase 3 video courses.

### Scenario B — Subscription (Magoosh-shaped)

- Free tier: 3 mock exam attempts/month, basic L'École access, 5 minutes of speaking practice/day
- Premium: $19.99/month or $99/year (40% saving)
- VIP: $79/month with 4 1-on-1 Zoom sessions with you

**Pros:** Highest LTV. Retains aligned with Phase 3 video content. Tier 3 puts you in product. Free tier acquires.
**Cons:** Higher friction at signup. Churn risk. More to manage.

### Scenario C — Hybrid one-time + add-on subscription (smart compromise)

- Core program: $99 one-time, 12-month access (matches PrepMyFuture)
- Live coaching add-on: $39/month, includes monthly group office hours + 2 private 30-min sessions
- VIP one-on-one tier: $499 for 8 sessions

**Pros:** Frictionless entry, premium upsell to MRR. PrepMyFuture price-comparable.
**Cons:** More complex pricing page. Two products to maintain.

### My recommendation: Scenario B with score guarantee

**Confidence: medium-high.**

Reasons:
1. Magoosh's ~$99-199 model is proven for test prep
2. MRR > one-time revenue for runway predictability
3. Free tier is the FluentLab/Promova counter-move
4. VIP tier puts your face front-and-center, justifies premium
5. Score guarantee turns "is this worth it?" into "what do I have to lose?"

**Concrete pricing:**
- Free: limited speaking practice, 1 mock exam/month, basic L'École access
- Premium: $19.99/month or $149/year (38% saving, ~$12.40/month equivalent)
- VIP: $79/month with 4 monthly Zoom sessions

**TCF score guarantee:** Complete L'École + 3 mock exams in 60 days. If no improvement, full refund (Premium tier only).

## The minimum defensible price

**$14.99/month or $99/year** is the absolute floor for a serious offering. Below that, you signal:
- This isn't a real product
- Buyer should expect generic content
- Customer support won't be there
- Brand positioning trends toward Duolingo (consumer language app), away from Magoosh (test prep)

You will lose Indian/Brazilian price-sensitive segment at $19.99. Geographic pricing solves this:
- $19.99/mo (or $149/yr) — North America, Europe, GCC
- $9.99/mo (or $79/yr) — India, Brazil, Vietnam, Egypt, Morocco, Philippines

Stripe + Paddle handle this transparently via IP-based pricing. Standard SaaS practice.

## What this means for the runway question

**Your goal stated yesterday: $100K/year from FluentPath within 12 months.**

Run the math at Scenario B pricing:

To hit $100K ARR:
- At $19.99/mo, you need 417 paying users
- At $149/yr, you need 671 yearly subscribers
- Mix scenario (60% monthly, 40% yearly): ~500 active paying users
- To get there, assume 5% conversion from free tier → 10,000 free users needed
- To get 10,000 free users: depends entirely on top-of-funnel marketing

**Realistic distribution paths:**
1. **Preply past students** — 50-100 direct, ~20 paying (~$4K ARR)
2. **YouTube content in French** — 12 months of consistent videos can deliver 5,000-15,000 subscribers in TCF niche (low competition)
3. **Arabic-language TikTok/Instagram** — 12 months of consistent posting in Darija/Arabic targeting Moroccan/Algerian/Tunisian/Egyptian audience can deliver another 5,000-10,000 followers
4. **English-language SEO** — "TCF speaking practice," "TCF Canada prep online," etc. takes 6+ months to rank, low competition
5. **Direct outreach via Reddit/Discord** — r/Canada_PR has 100K+ members, French-PR Discords have thousands. Allowed if value-driven.
6. **Meta ads** — at $20-50 CAC and $19.99/mo ARPU + 12-month avg LTV, ROAS works. But requires upfront capital.

**Honest assessment:** $100K/year in 12 months is achievable but **requires aggressive top-of-funnel work in parallel with product development.** Solo founder constraint is the bottleneck.

A more realistic 12-month target: **$50K-$75K ARR with 250-400 paying users**, providing strong validation for a year-2 push to $200K+ once you have product-market fit and can hire content/marketing help.

This is not bad news. It's just honest math.
